## 4. Key Themes

**Theme 1 — From Pilot to Production**

Agentic AI goes beyond GenAI, enabling autonomous action, workflow orchestration, and real-time decision-making at scale.
 
Lloyds Banking Group is leading the shift, applying agentic AI to customer interactions, operations, colleague enablement, and engineering.
 
NatWest, Lloyds, and Starling are working closely with the regulator as they test new services; NatWest is piloting agentic AI to speed complaints handling, and Starling plans to offer customers personalised budgeting tools capable of setting predictive spending caps and automating financial tasks.


**Theme 2 — Regulatory Principles Hold, but Edges Are Being Tested**

The FCA does not plan to introduce extra regulations for AI and will instead rely on existing frameworks.
 Yet 
firms have raised concerns about whether traditional model risk management and validation approaches can scale effectively in the context of widespread deployment of generative and agentic AI systems, and participants have questioned how the "human-in-the-loop" concept can be meaningfully applied as AI systems take on more decision-making functions.


**Theme 3 — Human-in-the-Loop Under Strain**

Members of the Bank of England's AI Consortium noted that maintaining a "human in the loop" may become increasingly strained as firms adopt agentic AI and move from back office to market-facing applications.
 
It was also suggested that for financial firms, access to and governance of underlying data is a more significant differentiator for ROI than speed of AI deployment.


**Theme 4 — Data Quality and Legacy Infrastructure as Adoption Barriers**

Most large financial services firms have grown through acquisition, leaving a patchwork of platforms and data models that were never designed to work together. That fragmentation limits what AI can actually do—if data is siloed, inconsistent or incorrectly classified, AI systems will produce unreliable outputs or surface information that introduces real risk.


**Theme 5 — Government Actively Accelerating Adoption**

Two leading industry figures have been appointed to spearhead the rollout of AI in financial services, as the government doubles down on seizing the opportunities of AI to drive growth, productivity and investment.
 
The two AI Champions are Harriet Rees (Group CIO of Starling Bank) and Dr Rohit Dhawan (Head of AI & Advanced Analytics at Lloyds Banking Group), reporting directly to the Economic Secretary to the Treasury.

## 5. Market Implications

**For Institutions:** 
Over the next three years, 57% of banking executives expect AI agents to be fully embedded in risk, compliance and audit functions, as well as fraud detection and transaction monitoring; another 56% believe AI agents will reach broad adoption in credit assessment, loan processing, and KYC.
 First-mover advantages are real but contested—institutions must prioritise data governance alongside deployment velocity.

**For Compliance & Risk:** 
The ICO identifies the use of agentic AI in financial services as being high risk
, and 
the ICO's report emphasises that agentic AI can both exacerbate existing data protection issues and introduce new ones—particularly as human oversight becomes more difficult. Despite this, organisations remain fully responsible for ensuring personal information is used appropriately.
 The SM&CR accountability chain will face novel stress-testing as autonomous agents enter advisory and transactional workflows.

**For Competitive Positioning:** 
The UK's comparatively advanced regulatory approach, including an AI sandbox and a new live-testing environment, has helped domestic lenders pull ahead of European peers in agentic AI development.
 
However, 99% of companies plan to put agents into production while only 11% have actually done so, owing to implementation challenges related to data, governance and security.


**For Vendors & Technology Providers:** 
The Treasury Committee has recommended that by end of 2026, HM Treasury must designate the major AI and cloud providers as Critical Third Parties.
 CTP designation would subject major hyperscalers and AI infrastructure providers to direct regulatory oversight—a material compliance and commercial risk.
