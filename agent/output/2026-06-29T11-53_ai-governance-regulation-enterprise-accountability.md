## 2. Executive Summary

## 3. Latest Developments

**① EU AI Act — Full Applicability Arrives 2 August 2026**


The EU AI Act entered into force on 1 August 2024 and will be fully applicable on 2 August 2026, with governance rules and GPAI obligations already active from August 2025. Transparency rules also take effect in August 2026.
 Critically, 
under the AI Act Omnibus — political agreement reached 7 May 2026 — Annex III high-risk obligations (covering recruitment tools, credit scoring, law enforcement, education, and border control) are deferred to 2 December 2027.

**② EU Cloud and AI Development Act (CADA) Proposed**


On 3 June 2026, the Commission adopted a proposal for the Cloud and AI Development Act (CADA), aimed at strengthening the EU's cloud and AI ecosystem, investment and infrastructure.
 
CADA responds to two concerns — a shortage of EU data centre capacity and reliance on a handful of non-EU cloud providers — and pairs supply-side measures with a contested cloud sovereignty framework built around four Union assurance levels.
 Industry groups are pushing back: 
the CCIA warns CADA introduces discriminatory measures by excluding trusted international technology providers based on headquarters location, forcing users to rely on a more limited selection of products.


*Source: European Commission (3 June 2026); CCIA Europe press release (3 June 2026); Hogan Lovells legal analysis (June 2026) | Evidence type: Primary + trade press | Confidence: **High***

**③ FCA Reframes Regulatory Posture for Agentic AI**


FCA CEO Nikhil Rathi, speaking at "Agents of Change — AI in UK Financial Services 2026" on 24 June 2026, signalled the FCA is rethinking what it means to be an effective regulator, with greater emphasis on competition, collaboration and system-wide risk awareness.
 
Rathi highlighted that the next phase will see agentic systems that do not just support financial decisions, but coordinate and transact — enabling personalised retail investment and wholesale liquidity management.
 He stressed that 
dependencies on model providers and third parties must be properly mapped and governed, and the Critical Third Parties regime becomes more important than ever.


*Source: FCA official speech transcript (24 June 2026) | Evidence type: Primary — regulator | Confidence: **High***

**④ South Africa Draft National AI Policy Gazetted**


The publication of South Africa's Draft National AI Policy in April 2026 marks an important shift from conceptual discussions on AI toward a structured national governance framework, laying down principles, institutions and implementation pathways.
 
Government has opted for a sector-specific, multi-regulator model with five core pillars: skills capacity, responsible governance, ethical and inclusive AI, cultural preservation and human-centred deployment.
 
The formal public consultation phase closed on 10 June 2026.


*Source: South African Government Gazette (10 April 2026); Baker McKenzie (26 Feb 2026); Adams & Adams (15 April 2026) | Evidence type: Primary — official government document | Confidence: **High***
