## 2. Executive Summary

The quarter ending July 2026 marks a structural turning point in AI governance across all three regions. In Europe, the Digital Omnibus agreement — formally approved by the EU Council on 29 June 2026 — has extended the high-risk AI compliance deadline from August 2026 to December 2027, providing 17 months of additional runway, but without removing the underlying obligations. The EU Commission simultaneously published its Cybersecurity & AI Action Plan on 7 July 2026. In the UK, binding AI legislation remains absent; governance is delivered through a patchwork of five sectoral regulators, with the FCA and Ofgem issuing the most active sector-specific guidance. In Africa, 16 nations now hold national AI strategies and Nigeria's Digital Economy Bill is approaching presidential assent, signalling a continental shift from aspirational policy to enforceable law. Across all sectors, a governance readiness gap is the defining strategic risk: only 8% of organisations globally hold a comprehensive AI governance framework, yet 88% actively deploy AI.

## 4. Key Themes

**Theme 1 — Deadline management is the immediate enterprise imperative.**

The core architecture of the AI Act — its risk-based classification, prohibited practices, and general-purpose AI rules — remains unchanged. Two categories of high-risk AI systems will now face extended application dates: high-risk AI systems listed in Annex III and deployed on a standalone basis will be subject to full compliance requirements as of 2 December 2027.
 The 17-month window is not a reprieve; it is a planning horizon.

**Theme 2 — Governance gaps remain dangerously wide.**

Only 8% of organisations globally have a comprehensive AI governance framework, yet 88% are actively using AI across business functions. That gap is where risk lives.
 In BFSI specifically, 
AI adoption is outpacing control structures, with only 30% of companies having responsible controls for current AI models even though nearly three-quarters have already integrated AI into initiatives.


**Theme 3 — Agentic AI is outpacing existing governance frameworks.**

The rapid deployment of agentic AI compounds cyber vulnerabilities, rendering manual oversight increasingly ineffective. Software engineering is the financial industry's most mature AI application (42% fully deployed), and is also a primary cyber risk transmission vector. 51% of respondents cite "loss of human oversight" as the third highest AI risk overall.


**Theme 4 — Africa's sovereignty agenda is reshaping governance design.**

Rwanda's Minister of ICT warned that Africa risks embedding inequality by continuing to rely on AI systems developed outside the continent. African nations have limited control over technologies shaping their economies, healthcare, and governance. AI tools trained on foreign datasets can misdiagnose diseases and misread economic trends. "AI sovereignty" — a country's ability to design, develop, and govern its own AI systems — must be the goal.

## 5. Market Implications

- **BFSI:** 
A bank that purchases a third-party credit scoring AI becomes a deployer with obligations around human oversight, monitoring, and incident reporting. Deployers remain liable if they modify the AI system's intended purpose or fail to use it according to the provider's instructions.
 The FSB's 12-practice framework — open for consultation until 22 July 2026 — will likely set the global compliance floor for the sector.

- **Energy:** 
Since publishing its initial strategic approach to AI in 2024, Ofgem has taken steps to advance its framework for safe and ethical AI use. Whilst Ofgem maintains that the existing regulatory framework is appropriate, it issued additional guidance in May 2025 on ethical AI deployment, focusing on governance, transparency and sustainability principles for licensees.


- **Technology / CMT / cross-sector:** 
To promote safe use of advanced AI, the Commission will strengthen Europe's capacity to evaluate AI models before they are placed on the EU market. It will also work with ENISA to establish a secure testing platform to help organisations in critical sectors safely test and deploy AI solutions.
 Pre-market scrutiny is becoming the operative model; procurement timelines and vendor onboarding must adapt.

- **Africa — BFSI & Pharma:** 
45 African nations have enacted data protection legislation, with 39 regulatory authorities fully operational. Regulators are increasingly mandating Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs) and Algorithmic Impact Assessments (AIAs), elevating the cost of non-compliance and making institutional-grade governance non-negotiable.
