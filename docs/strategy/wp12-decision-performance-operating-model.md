## 0. The organising principle

> **TheAIReadyist helps organisations become AI Ready by improving _Decision Performance_ through _Decision Intelligence_ — using _Constraint Intelligence_ to find the one limit that matters most.**

Everything below is an expression of that sentence. The test for any future asset, page, assessment or feature is simple: **does it measurably improve Decision Performance (or apply the discipline / engine that does)? If not, it does not belong on the platform.**

### The four concepts, by altitude (how complexity is controlled)

The strategic risk this work package addresses is *too many competing concepts*. They only compete if they share an altitude. They do not:

```
   ALTITUDE        CONCEPT                  ROLE                        MARKET-FACING
   ─────────       ───────                  ────                        ─────────────
   Mission     →   AI Readiness             the promise / front door    YES — the hook
   Category    →   Decision Performance     what we own and sell        YES — the category
   Engine      →   Constraint Intelligence  the proprietary mechanism   NO  — the moat
   Discipline  →   Decision Intelligence    internal method / heritage  NO  — rarely surfaced
```

**The market only has to learn two ideas: become _AI Ready_ by improving your _Decision Performance_.** Constraint Intelligence and Decision Intelligence sit below the waterline. This is a reduction in conceptual load, not an increase.

## D1 — Canonical Concept Hierarchy

### Definitions

- **AI Readiness** *(mission / destination).* An organisation's capability to make the right decisions *about* AI, *with* AI, and *through* AI such that AI produces reliable value at scale. Not technology adoption. It is the board-level goal and the demand the market already expresses.
- **Decision Intelligence** *(discipline / mechanism).* The discipline of improving organisational decisions through data, context, governance, automation and AI. It is **a discipline operationalised as a methodology — deliberately not the category** (contested; see D6). It is the *how*.
- **Decision Performance** *(category / measurable outcome).* An organisation's ability to consistently make high-value decisions at the right speed, with appropriate accountability, supported by trusted intelligence and effective execution. **DI is the input; DP is the output.** It decomposes into six measurable dimensions, which is what makes it ownable as a category.
- **Constraint Intelligence** *(proprietary engine).* The mechanism that identifies, classifies and aggregates the single organisational limitation most reducing Decision Performance — across one org (via the DPI) and across the market (via Signals). The moat. See D5.
- **Business Outcomes** *(value).* What Decision Performance compounds into: growth, resilience, efficiency, innovation, competitiveness.

### Canonical hierarchy

```
   GOAL    │  AI READINESS                  the executive is buying readiness
           │  "right decisions about / with / through AI, at scale"
           │        │ is achieved by
   ────────┼──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   CATEGORY│  DECISION PERFORMANCE          what TheAIReadyist owns & measures
           │  6 dimensions: Quality · Velocity · Accountability ·
           │  Capability · Value · Resilience
           │        ▲ produced by              ▲ diagnosed by
   ────────┼───────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────
   ENGINE  │  DECISION INTELLIGENCE     │  CONSTRAINT INTELLIGENCE
   ROOM    │  (discipline) applied      │  (proprietary engine) finds the
           │  through the Six Pillars / │  one binding limit, per org and
           │  Decision Systems          │  across the market
           │        │ compounds into
   ────────┼──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   VALUE   │  BUSINESS OUTCOMES   growth · resilience · efficiency ·
           │                      innovation · competitiveness
```

**Validation of the brief's proposed hierarchy** (`AI Readiness ↓ Decision Intelligence ↓ Decision Performance ↓ Business Outcomes`): **validated with two tightenings** — (1) AI Readiness is the *destination achieved by* the layers below, not a causal antecedent of Decision Intelligence (your `01` doc states DI is the *prerequisite for* readiness); (2) Decision Intelligence and Constraint Intelligence are demoted out of the market-facing chain into the "engine room," because Decision Performance — being measurable — is the layer that can actually be owned as a category.

### How Decision Performance translates to Business Outcomes

| Outcome | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| Growth | Quality + Value → better bets, capital to decisions that demonstrably pay off |
| Resilience | Resilience dimension → decisions hold under regulatory/geopolitical/technical stress |
| Efficiency | Velocity → less rework, fewer escalation loops, lower decision latency |
| Innovation | Velocity + Capability → more good decisions, made faster |
| Competitiveness | All six compounding → a decision advantage rivals can't buy with the same AI |

## D3 — Proprietary Asset Analysis

| Tier | Definition | Assets |
|---|---|---|
| **Commodity** | Anyone can build | EU AI Act / DORA / NIS2 assessments; raw signal feeds; generic checklists |
| **Differentiated** | Harder to replicate; erodes | Signals *as DP/constraint-classified*; Intelligence Briefing; Governance Radar; Six-Pillar taxonomy |
| **Proprietary** | Category-defining; compounding | **DP methodology**; **DPI**; **Constraint Intelligence engine**; **Benchmarks**; **datasets**; **research corpus** |

**Moat test:** does it (a) compound with use via data network effects, (b) rest on defensible methodology IP, (c) accrue authority capital? Ranked by strategic value (judgement):

1. **Decision Performance methodology** — the source code of the category.
2. **DPI** — methodology made instrument; the conversion engine and dataset generator. *Invest here first.*
3. **Benchmarks (future)** — DPI responses → comparative dataset with classic network effects. *Biggest future moat.*
4. **Constraint Intelligence (engine)** — aggregated constraints across signals + assessments. *Category-building evidence; gated on volume.*
5. **Research corpus (future)** — converts data + methodology into analyst-grade authority.
6. **Signals (DP-classified)** — differentiated only via the classification layer; defend by deepening classification, not adding feeds.
7. **Six-Pillar taxonomy** — organising IP; enables the above.
8. **Briefing / Radar** — useful for engagement; replicable.
9. **Compliance assessments** — commodity; **harvest as funnel, never as category** (ratified position).

**The uncomfortable truth the model must force:** the platform's most *visible* assets are its least *defensible*; its most *defensible* assets barely exist yet. Therefore the single strategic imperative is **credible DPI volume**, because DPI completions feed benchmarks, constraint intelligence and research simultaneously. Every roadmap decision should be scored on *"does this drive credible DPI volume?"*

**Decisive insight:** signals and assessments are *two feeds into one constraint taxonomy*. Shared vocabulary lets the platform say what no competitor can — *"your primary constraint is the 2nd most common in your sector this quarter."* That sentence is the moat in miniature, and it requires unifying the taxonomy **now**, before either dataset scales.

## D5 — Constraint Intelligence Framework (the engine)

Per the ratified decision, Constraint Intelligence is the **proprietary engine** beneath the Decision Performance category — surfaced selectively as signature methodology, not sold as the category.

**What constitutes a constraint:** the single organisational limitation that most reduces an entity's Decision Performance — the binding limit that, if relieved, most improves the ability to make high-value decisions at pace, with accountability. (The DPI's existing "primary constraint", generalised to signals and to the market.)

**Two-level taxonomy** — *closed* at Level 1, *open* at Level 2:

| L1 Constraint Domain (= DP dimension) | L2 Named constraint types |
|---|---|
| Quality | Data Fragmentation; Bias Amplification; Implicit Decision Architecture |
| Velocity | Decision Latency; Authority Conflict; Operating-Model Lag |
| Accountability | Accountability Gap; Governance Ambiguity; Audit-Trail Deficit |
| Capability | Capability Deficit; Judgement Atrophy; Adoption Theatre |
| Value | Economic Uncertainty; Attribution Failure; Cost Opacity |
| Resilience | Single-Provider Dependency; Regulatory Exposure; Data-Residency Risk |

**Severity (by scope):** organisation = gap-to-benchmark × leverage (DPI); single signal = `decision_performance_impact` (Low→Critical); market = frequency × impact-weight over a rolling window (min-sample guardrails).

**Aggregation:** per-org *constraint profile* (primary + secondary + trend); cohort *benchmarks*; market *Constraint Index*.

**Relationships:** constraints *are* DP expressed as its binding limit (↔ Decision Performance); the DPI is the measurement instrument that produces an org's L1 constraint (↔ DPI); signals are the market sensor producing L2 observations (↔ Signals); narrower assessments add domain detail and funnel to the DPI (↔ Assessments).

### Constraint Intelligence Operating Model

```
 INSTRUMENTS                 TAXONOMY                AGGREGATION             PRODUCTS
 DPI ──────────► L1 primary ─┐
 Assessments ──► domain gaps ─┼─► UNIFIED ──► per-org profile ──► Diagnosis + Advisory
 Signals ──────► L2 named ────┘   CONSTRAINT     cohort rollup ──► Benchmarks
                                  TAXONOMY       market rollup ──► Constraint Index + Research
                                  (6 × N · closed L1 · open L2 · severity by scope)
```

**Architectural instruction:** own the unified constraint taxonomy as **platform master data**, not as a signals feature. This is the decision that converts a collection of assets into a Decision Performance observatory. (Build gated on volume — see WP11 D6; revisit at ~100–150 published signals / sufficient DPI completions.)

## D6 — Category Ownership Strategy

### Candidate evaluation

| Candidate | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| **Decision Performance** | **PRIMARY CATEGORY** | Measurable, ownable, executive-legible, brand-aligned, white space |
| **AI Readiness** | **SECONDARY / discovery** | Real demand + the brand name, but commoditising and not ownable as a category → use as mission and funnel |
| Decision Intelligence | Reject (as category) | Contested; owned by Gartner/Google/vendors. Keep as internal discipline |
| Executive Decision Systems | Reject | Architectural/internal; not a category executives shop for |
| Decision Readiness | Reject | Invented hybrid; overlaps Readiness without Performance's measurability |
| Operational Decision Intelligence | Reject | Narrow, BI/ops-coded; sounds like an analytics niche |

### Naming decision tree

```
 For any candidate category label, ask in order:
 1. Does it capture existing executive demand?
 2. Is it ownable (not already claimed by Gartner/Google/vendors)?
 3. Is it executive-legible in <30 seconds, with a positive frame?
 4. Can products + subscriptions sit naturally beneath it?

 AI Readiness            → 1:YES  2:NO              → MISSION / front door (not the category)
 Decision Intelligence   → 1:YES  2:NO              → internal discipline (reject as category)
 Decision Performance    → 1:MOD  2:YES 3:YES 4:YES → ★ CATEGORY
 Constraint Intelligence → 1:NO   3:borderline      → ENGINE (reject as category; publish as methodology)
 Exec Decision Systems / Decision Readiness / ODI   → fail 2/3/4 → reject
```

### Recommendation

- **Primary category:** **Decision Performance.** TheAIReadyist is *"the Decision Performance platform."*
- **Secondary category (discovery):** **AI Readiness** — the demand-capture layer that funnels into Decision Performance.
- **Proprietary engine (named, not sold):** **Constraint Intelligence.**
- **Discipline (internal):** Decision Intelligence.

### Supporting language (lexicon)

| Use | Avoid / de-emphasise |
|---|---|
| Decision Performance; primary constraint; decision system; six dimensions | "AI maturity"; "AI adoption score" |
| Decision Performance Index (DPI); benchmark; constraint profile | "Decision Intelligence platform" (cedes to Gartner/Google) |
| Constraint Intelligence (as our engine/methodology) | "Constraint Intelligence" as the headline product/category |
| AI-ready / AI Readiness (as the goal) | Readiness as a *measurable score* (Performance is what's measured) |

### Positioning statement

> **TheAIReadyist is the Decision Performance platform for the AI era.** We help enterprise leaders become AI-ready by measuring and improving how their organisation decides — across six dimensions: quality, velocity, accountability, capability, value and resilience. The Decision Performance Index identifies the single constraint most limiting performance; Executive Signals show how the changing world stresses it; benchmarks and research show how you compare and where to act.

Short form: *"Most AI failures are Decision Performance failures. We help you find — and fix — the one constraint that matters most."*

### The brand–category drift, named (not buried)

The name *TheAIReadyist* anchors on the **mission** layer (Readiness) while the **owned category** is one layer down (Performance). This is a manageable tension, not a flaw: **Readiness is the front door; Decision Performance is the room.** Recommendation: **keep the name** (equity + captures real search demand) and make *"the Decision Performance platform"* the consistent category descriptor in all positioning. Do **not** rename to a Decision Performance brand — that would forfeit the demand the current name captures. Revisit only if category education proves harder than expected.

## D7 — Platform North Star (3-year vision)

### What the platform becomes

**The system of record for enterprise Decision Performance** — the recognised place where executives diagnose the single constraint most limiting their decisions, benchmark it against peers, track how the changing world stresses it, and prove improvement over time. The category-defining authority on the idea that *most AI failures are Decision Performance failures.*

### Staged path

| Horizon | Focus | Proof point |
|---|---|---|
| **Year 1 — Instrument & funnel** | DPI as flagship; compliance assessments + signals as funnel; unify constraint taxonomy | Credible DPI completion volume; signals live and DP-classified |
| **Year 2 — Benchmark & engine** | Benchmarks from DPI cohort data; Constraint Intelligence engine live; first research | "How do you compare" becomes real; first cited Constraint Index |
| **Year 3 — Authority & indispensability** | Longitudinal tracking; research corpus; advisory; analyst recognition of the category | Re-assessment cohorts; press/analyst citation; renewal-driven revenue |

### The flywheel (why it compounds)

```
   more DPI completions ─► richer benchmarks ─► more credible diagnosis ─►
        ▲                                                                │
        │                                                                ▼
   authority & research ◄─ larger constraint corpus ◄─ more signals + assessments
```

### What executives use it for
Diagnose their primary constraint; benchmark against peers/sector/size; monitor how external developments stress their decision system; track improvement across re-assessments; brief their board with credible, comparable evidence.

### What data it owns
The proprietary DPI response dataset; the unified constraint corpus (org + market); longitudinal re-assessment trends; cohort benchmarks. **Aggregate, non-personal, and accumulated over time — the part competitors cannot buy.**

### What insights it generates
Per-org constraint diagnosis; sector/size/geo benchmarks; the market Constraint Index ("the constraints enterprises keep hitting"); longitudinal shifts; research-grade findings.

### What makes it indispensable
- **Switching cost:** your history, benchmarks and trend live here; leaving resets the baseline.
- **Comparability:** the peer benchmark only exists because of the shared dataset — unavailable elsewhere.
- **Authority:** the platform defines the language executives and analysts use for the problem.

### What competitors cannot easily replicate
The accumulated DPI dataset, the unified constraint corpus, longitudinal trends, and the research authority built on them. A competitor can copy a questionnaire or a news feed in a week; they cannot copy three years of proprietary Decision Performance data or the category language it earned. **The methodology is publishable; the dataset and the authority are not transferable.**
